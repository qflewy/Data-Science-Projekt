Marine cargo traffic and logistics in the European market is a rich and impactful area for a data science project, 
especially given the availability of public datasets from sources like Eurostat, EMODnet (European Marine Observation and Data Network), UNCTAD (United Nations Conference on Trade and Development), and Port Authorities. 
Here are some research questions and angles you could explore, along with the types of data you might use:

1. Efficiency and Congestion in European Ports
Research Questions:
* Which European ports experience the highest congestion, and what are the seasonal or annual patterns?
* How do delays in major ports (e.g., Rotterdam, Hamburg, Antwerp) correlate with global events (e.g., pandemics, geopolitical tensions, or climate-related disruptions)?
* Can machine learning models predict port congestion based on historical traffic, weather, and economic indicators?
Data Sources:
* Eurostat: Port traffic statistics, container throughput, and vessel calls.
* EMODnet: Real-time and historical vessel density maps.
* Port Authority Reports: Publicly available reports from major ports.

2. Environmental Impact of Marine Cargo Traffic
Research Questions:
* What is the carbon footprint of cargo ships in European waters, and how has it changed over the past decade?
* How do emission control areas (ECAs) in Europe affect shipping routes and fuel consumption?
* Can we identify "green" shipping corridors or ports with lower emissions?
Data Sources:
* Eurostat: Emissions from maritime transport.
* EMODnet: Vessel tracking data (AIS) for route analysis.
* EU MRV Regulation Data: Monitoring, Reporting, and Verification of CO2 emissions from ships.

3. Trade Flow Analysis and Economic Impact
Research Questions:
* Which European countries are the largest importers/exporters by sea, and what are the dominant commodities?
* How have trade flows shifted due to Brexit, the Ukraine war, or the Suez Canal disruptions?
* Can we predict trade volume changes based on macroeconomic indicators (e.g., GDP, inflation)?
Data Sources:
* Eurostat: International trade in goods by maritime transport.
* UNCTAD: Global and regional trade statistics.
* World Bank: Economic indicators for correlation analysis.

4. Supply Chain Resilience and Risk Assessment
Research Questions:
* Which European ports are most vulnerable to supply chain disruptions (e.g., strikes, natural disasters)?
* How do alternative routes (e.g., Arctic shipping) impact the resilience of European supply chains?
* Can we model the risk of delays or bottlenecks in the European maritime network?
Data Sources:
* Eurostat: Port infrastructure and connectivity data.
* EMODnet: Historical data on disruptions (e.g., storms, accidents).
* Lloyd’s List Intelligence: Publicly available risk reports.

5. Digitalization and Automation in Ports
Research Questions:
* How are European ports adopting automation (e.g., automated cranes, AI-driven logistics), and what is the impact on efficiency?
* What is the correlation between digitalization levels and port performance metrics (e.g., turnaround time, throughput)?
* Can we identify best practices for digital transformation in ports?
Data Sources:
* Eurostat: Port infrastructure and technology adoption surveys.
* Port Authority Whitepapers: Public reports on digitalization projects.
* EU Research Projects: Horizon 2020/Horizon Europe reports on smart ports.

6. Impact of Geopolitical Events on Shipping Routes
Research Questions:
* How have sanctions, conflicts, or trade wars (e.g., Russia-Ukraine, US-China) altered shipping routes to/from Europe?
* What are the economic costs of rerouting cargo due to geopolitical risks?
* Can we predict future route changes based on geopolitical trends?
Data Sources:
* EMODnet: AIS data for route analysis.
* UNCTAD: Reports on geopolitical impacts on trade.
* News Archives: For event timelines (e.g., Reuters, BBC).

7. Forecasting Cargo Demand
Research Questions:
* Can we forecast cargo demand for specific European ports using time series analysis?
* How do external factors (e.g., oil prices, global demand) influence cargo volumes?
* What is the accuracy of different forecasting models (e.g., ARIMA, LSTM) for port traffic?
Data Sources:
* Eurostat: Historical cargo volumes.
* World Bank: Commodity price indices.
* IMF: Global economic outlook reports.

Data Accessibility Notes
* Eurostat is your best friend for structured, reliable, and free data. Use their database and filter by "maritime transport."
* EMODnet provides AIS data and environmental layers (e.g., wind, waves) that can be combined with traffic data.
* Port Authorities (e.g., Rotterdam, Hamburg) often publish annual reports with detailed statistics.
